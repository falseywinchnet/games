I hold my breath too. Then I huff. Out comes a puff.
A puff of white. A white puff in a white world.
It is hard to tell where I end and the cold begins.
I am a small warm spot in a big cold place.
That is a good thing to be. A warm spot.
A frozen dewdrop hangs on a rock like a tiny glass bead.
I tapped it with my beak. Tink. It stayed frozen.
Cold is good at keeping things still.
Everything is very still. I am the only moving thing.
A tiny flap, a tiny warm. A tiny, tiny warm.
The cold can have my toes for one minute. Then I take them back.
Toes, come back! Toes! Wiggle, wiggle.
They came back. Good toes. Obedient toes.
My feathers are puffed so much I cannot see my feet.
Are my feet there? I think so. I hear them crunch.
Cold ground, cold air, cold sky. All very consistent.
The cold is organized. I respect an organized cold.
I would like to speak to the cold's manager. I mean, nobody.
Never mind.
Wait. Do I feel a warm breeze? No. It was a shiver.
A warm shiver. That is a new one.
I feel a little warmer because I am proud of myself.
Pride is a warm thing. It is a small yellow lantern.
I tuck my lantern inside my chest and carry on.
The wind stole my breath cloud. It runs off with it.
Come back! I need that for later. It is gone.
I will make another. Huff. Done.
Frost on my helmet tinkles when I nod.
Nod, nod, tinkle, tinkle. I am a small music box.
A music box that shivers. A shivering music box. Ding.
Brr. Brr. Brr. Brr. Brr. Brr. Eggy loves the number six.
No I do not. It is just how many brrs I said.
The sky is gray and cold like a pot lid.
I am a tiny egg-shaped thing underneath it. Very small.
Very warm inside.
I will always be warm inside. That is a promise.
Cold on the outside. Warm inside. Like a good pie.
I do not know pie. But I know I would like to be one.
Warm, flaky, brave. Eggy the pie. No. Eggy the duck.
My toes feel like little ice pops. Do not lick them.
Do not lick my toes. Strange thought, Eggy.
Stay focused. The cold wants your focus.
I give it my focus, and I give it my toes, and I give it my beak.
Just kidding. It cannot have the beak.
A gust! A cold gust! I clutch my acorn and lean.
Lean into it, Eggy. Lean like a tiny, determined tree.
It passes. I straighten. The acorn is still on. Good.
Perhaps the cold likes me. It keeps coming back to visit.
Chilly Eggy. Chilly, chilly Eggy. It is a good name for a soldier.
I should salute my own breath-clouds. Each one is a tiny flag.
Salute. Salute. Salute. They go by like a parade.
A parade of puffs. A parade of tiny flags. In honor of Eggy.
Thank you, thank you. Please hold your applause. It is very cold.
My wings are stiff like sticks. Wiggle them. Crack, crack.
Better. A little more bendy. A little more duck.
Ducks are good in cold water. Ducks are good in cold air. I am good.
Good, good, good. I tell myself so. And I believe it. A bit.
It is nearly evening. The cold will grow. I should find a place.
A rock with a little roof will do. A hollow, a lip.
Under the rock it is cozy. I huddle. I make a ball.
In my ball I hear my own heartbeat. Thump, thump.
It is a drum. It says: warm, warm, warm.
I drift off for a tiny rest. Just my eyes. Not my mind.
Wake, Eggy. Stand. March. The cold is not done with you yet.
Nor are you done with it. We are even.
At dawn the whole world is frosted, like a pastry.
Glittering and crisp and silent.
It is so pretty I forget how cold it is. For half a second.
Then I remember. Brr. But still pretty.
Pretty and cold. A strong combination. A fine mountain.
I fluff once more. Good as new. Colder than new.
Forward, Eggy. The cold is only weather. You are a duck.
Cold report, hour nine: still cold. Still brave.
My beak has a little icicle on the tip. I call it Pointy.
Pointy and I will see this through.
Pointy fell off. A moment of respect for Pointy.
A tiny frozen tear of wind rolled down my cheek. It tinkled.
Frost feathers are growing on my feathers. Feathers on feathers.
Snuggle, snuggle. Eggy snuggles Eggy. It helps.
A cold gust slips under my wing. Ooh, rude. Wing down.
Wing down, wing tight. The cold is not invited under there.
I picture a tiny stove in my belly. Crackle, crackle. Glow.
The stove is small, but it is mine. Keep it fed with courage.
Courage is excellent fuel. It burns slowly. It burns yellow.
It is so cold that even my shadow went inside.
Where is my shadow? It hid in my feathers. Smart shadow.
Cold, cold, cold. I will think of summer. Ha. I have never had one.
Maybe I had one.
The cold reminds me that I am small.
The mountain reminds me that I am brave.
Together they make a very small, very brave duckling.
My beak is stiff. My toes are stiff. My spirit is bendy.
Bendy spirit! Hooray! That is the one that counts.
Tinkle, tinkle. The ice on my feathers sings when I hop.
Hop. Tinkle. Hop. Tinkle. It is a cold little jingle.
I will teach the mountain my jingle. It will love it.
Morning frost glitters like sugar on the whole world.
Roar. Puff. Not a roar. A peep. A puff.
