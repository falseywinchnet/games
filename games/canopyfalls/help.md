Canopy Falls is a living waterfall. You stand on a wooden walkway high at the edge of the rainforest, looking out over a lily pool in a clearing at one broad curtain of a great river pouring forty metres over a basalt cliff. The sun stands high above its brink. There is nothing to win; it is there to be looked at and listened to.

The water never repeats. It leaves the brink as a glassy, golden sheet where the river is thin, breaks into white fingers, then falls as torn ropes and clumps of white water, surging heavier and lighter, throwing drops and catching the sun's glints all the way down, and boils up as spray where it meets the pool. The sun glitters on the pool's ripples, and you can see down through its water to the stones on the bottom.

Space (or Pause in the menu) stops the falls exactly where they are. A paused scene does no work at all. The scene also stops whenever another game or the shelf is in front.

Detail (Q, or Settings) chooses the size of the scene's pixels and how much spray the falls throw: Fine is sharpest, Light is gentlest on an older or busy computer, Balanced sits between. Whatever the detail, the scene measures what it costs and draws a little less often rather than take more than its share of the processor. When the window changes size, the new picture is made in the background while the old one stays up.

The roar of the falls, the churn at their foot, the pool lapping at the walkway's piles, the birds, insects and frogs of the forest, and the wind in the canopy are sound, under Sound, made as they play. A harp, softly stroked, with a celesta, tuned wind chimes and an airy pad, is music, under Music, and never plays the same way twice.

With Reduced Motion on, the water falls at half speed. Everything is drawn by the processor; no graphics card is needed.
