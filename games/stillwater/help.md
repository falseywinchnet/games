Stillwater is a living aquarium: a planted tank of tall grass leaning in a slow current, a driftwood branch furred with moss, dark boulders and pebbles on pale sand, sixteen silver tetras, two crabs and a little sunken treasure chest, its lid ajar over a heap of gold. Sunlight through the rippling surface throws a slowly shifting net of bright lines over the sand, the stones, the leaves and the fish: crisp near the surface, softer on the floor. There is nothing to win; it is there to be looked at.

Choose the tank in Settings, or press S for the next one; it changes through a moment of dark water: the planted tank; a coral reef of live rock crowded with table, staghorn, brain, finger and plate corals, sponges, sea fans and anemones, with a cloud of green chromis, a school of orange anthias, yellow and blue tangs, clownfish keeping to their anemones and a hermit crab by the chest; and a dim river pool, tea-coloured water under tree roots, sunbeams, sunken branches, leaf litter and eelgrass, with a shoal of minnows, a few red-finned rudd and darters on the gravel. Stillwater remembers the tank you chose.

Click the glass near a fish to startle it. It darts away from your finger, then settles into a new loop where it landed. The crabs shuffle a little sideways. Each tap makes a soft fingertip knock on the glass.

Space (or Pause in the menu) stops the tank exactly where it is. A paused tank does no work at all, and Resume continues from the same moment. The tank also stops whenever another game or the shelf is in front.

Detail (Q, or Settings) chooses the size of the tank's pixels: Fine is sharper, Light is gentlest on an older or busy computer, Balanced sits between. Whatever the detail, the tank measures what it costs and slows its swimming and swaying a little rather than take more than its share of the processor, and it slows further while another window is in front.

The filter's hum, the water, the air stone's bubbles and the knock on the glass are sound, under Sound, and kept faint. Somewhere an island band is improvising lazy tunes on an old record: a lap steel sliding into the melody, a ukulele strumming, an upright bass, vibraphone and marimba answering, a shaker and a few bubbles. They are music, under Music, and M turns them off and on.

With Reduced Motion on, the grass and the light on the water hold still; the fish keep swimming. Everything is drawn by the processor; no graphics card is needed.
