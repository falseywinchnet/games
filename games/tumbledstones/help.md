Every stone in the tray is one of a few shapes, each cut from any mineral: the mineral
never matters, only the shape and which way it lies. Your goal is the pull you chose at
the start: one single group of that many stones.

**Turning a stone.** Click a stone to pick it up and turn it over; click again to turn
it over again. Right-click (or press R) to turn it a quarter round. Move the pointer
away, or press Space, to set it down.

**Pulling.** When you set a stone down, it and every stone joined to it, side by side
or above and below, that is the same shape lying exactly the same way, form a group.
Two or more are pulled from the tray. Stones above fall into the gaps, keeping how
they lie, and new stones drop in from the top. What they form is pulled only when you
set a stone down among it; picking a stone up and putting it back as it was pulls a
group that is already there.

**Swapping.** Drag a stone onto a neighbour to swap them. Each stone may swap once;
a small brass pin marks the stones that have. The marks clear when the tray is juggled.

**Juggling.** When no match is left the tray juggles itself: the stones are thrown up,
turned and set down anew. Choose Juggle (J) when you are stuck. Families of stones run
out and new ones come in, sometimes near relations of those already there.

**The score.** Every move counts one; every pull costs points, fewer the larger it is
(100 for two). The game ends when one pull reaches your size. For pulls of seven or
more the lowest moves plus points is your best.

**Keys.** Arrows move; F turns over; R turns round; Space or Enter sets down; Shift
and an arrow swaps; Escape puts a stone back as it was; J juggles; N starts a new game.
