# Audio Manifest — Card Games + Puzzle family

All audio is original and generated by the scripts in `work/src/` (numpy synthesis: FM, additive/modal, wavetable, filtered noise, synthetic convolution reverb). **No samples, recordings or third-party audio assets are used**, so there are no external assets to list in a provenance record. Reference recordings were analysed numerically only. No audio from them is in any output.

Regenerate: `cd work/src && python3 render_music.py && python3 sfx.py && python3 finalize.py` (Python 3 + numpy, ffmpeg with libopus and aac_at). Seeds are fixed, so output is reproducible on the same numpy version.

## Runtime encodings

For every WAV there is `outputs/runtime/<name>.ogg` (Opus; 112 kb/s for music, 64 kb/s otherwise) and `<name>.m4a` (AAC, Apple encoder; 160 kb/s / 96 kb/s). **Compressed files add encoder delay and padding, so they do not loop sample-accurately in every player.** For gapless music either loop the WAV, decode the whole file to a PCM buffer and loop it using the sample counts above (trimming any decoder priming), or use an engine with gapless Opus or AAC support.

## Implementation notes

- Masters: music −20 LUFS integrated, peaks ≤ −4 dBTP. Suggested in-game music gain is about 0.4 (≈ −8 dB) with SFX at 1.0, which leaves card ticks clearly on top. Duck music by about 6 dB under stingers, or stop music and play the stinger.
- Frequent SFX have round-robin variants (pickup/place/deal/flip ×4, walk ×4, push ×3). Pick randomly without immediate repeats. A ±1 semitone pitch jitter is optional.
- `card_foundation_01..04` rise G→A→B→D (vibes fifths). Step through them on consecutive foundation moves and reset after about 2 s idle.
- Puzzle: `puzzle_walk` per step. `puzzle_push` replaces the walk sound when a block moves (it ends with a landing thunk around 0.4 s). Use `puzzle_blocked` for the comic bonk-and-spring when movement fails.
- Validation (in `audio_manifest.json`): zero clipped samples, DC < 1e-6, and the loop-seam sample jump sits within the normal sample-to-sample range (click ratio < 1).

## Reference analysis (measured vs inferred)

Numerical analysis only (`work/src/analyze_reference*.py`, results in `work/analysis/*.json`, spectrograms `*.png`). **I did not hear these recordings.** Everything below comes from signal measurements, plus my reading of the spectrogram images, which is labelled as inferred.

**Crazy8 in-game music (card-game reference).** Measured: 175.8 s; ~120 BPM (beat-phase fit); offbeat onsets cluster around 0.62–0.71 of the beat, i.e. a shuffle/swing feel; pitch-class profile fits A minor / C major (r = 0.48 / 0.43); 72 % of energy is below 400 Hz, only about 4 % above 1.5 kHz; side/mid −6.5 dB; level fairly steady after the opening 8 s. Inferred: warm, bass-forward, gently swung and relaxed. My response: swung or bouncy grooves at 84–150 BPM, mellow mallets, EP and nylon timbres, soft brushes, and moderate brightness (I added a +3 dB presence shelf so cards still read clearly over the music).

**Catching Thieves loops 179 and 165 (puzzle reference).** Measured: both mono, sample rates 11.025 / 22.05 kHz. 179 is strongly G major (r = 0.92), with bass mostly on G and D (tonic/dominant oom-pah); strongest periodicity is ~132 BPM, with 69.8 also a candidate; 71 % of energy is at 120–400 Hz. 165 has no clear key (C/E-minor-ish chroma, bass on F/C), tempo candidates 117–136 BPM, and heavy energy below 400 Hz. Inferred from spectrograms: 179 has sustained harmonic-rich reed- or organ-like tones over a repeating bass figure; 165 shows clear pitch glides in the lead (slide-whistle-like) over a booming bass. My response: an original G-major oom-pah (`puzzle_main`: tuba-like bass, accordion-like reed off-beats, woodblocks, reed/marimba/slide-whistle leads) and a C-major tiptoe loop with a glide lead. No melodies, chords or rhythms were transcribed. Tempi and key centres are broad, non-protectable parameters. The recovered tracks and SFX are not included and must not ship; their rights are not established.

## Not verified

- **Nobody has listened to these files yet**, me included: I cannot hear audio. Musical quality, mix balance, and how 'cheerful' or 'tiring' they feel are unconfirmed and need a human listening pass. Melodies are generated from authored motifs, rhythms and chord charts, so expect some phrases to need re-seeding or editing (change `seed` in `songs.py`).
- SFX loudness relative to music is set by numbers, not ears. Tune per-event gains in the engine.
- Opus/AAC loop behaviour has not been tested inside the target game engine.
