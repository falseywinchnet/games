GAME ZENCONSTRUCTION 01 — Zen Construction, a new game for the collection

A rock-stacking game: a Jenga of river stones. It was built on the Neo by Claude Opus, working with the user, from the user's brief of 2026-10-03, borrowing the Eggy, Switchbox and Pen the Sheep techniques (software 3D, crisp text layers, synthesized audio). It has its own rigid-body engine, written to Claude's specification (PHYSICS_SPEC.md) by a Fable session at the user's request and then optimized here. The user played it, directed it closely, and asked for it to go into the collection on 2026-10-03. It replaces nothing: it needs a new collection entry.

A little yellow 1960s-style toy truck crane sits perched on a boulder on a rock bank by a brook. Mina, an anime girl in a hard hat, works the levers in the operator's cab; a rubber duck in an orange hard hat rides at the truck's wheel. Beside it is a turned wooden bowl of about fifty generated stones (flat river discs, slabs, blocks, jagged shards, a few cobbles, each made of one of eight stones: pink and grey granite, sandstone, slate, basalt, quartzite, limestone, greywacke). Click a rock and the crane fetches it, wraps two orange web slings round it and lifts it straight up, slowly, clear of everything. Then the player works the crane: up/down arrows telescope the boom out and in, left/right swing it, W/S pay the line out and in, Q/E turn the rock, R/F tip it toward or away from the crane, Z/C roll it, Shift for fine work, Space lets go, B carries it back to the bowl. Set it down on the bedrock ledge, then stack the next on it: the score is the height of the stack. The first rock set down is the stack's foot; a rock joins the stack while it rests, still, on one below it. Only a rock with nothing on it can be lifted, so a stack comes apart one rock at a time; rocks that fall are tidied back into the bowl by themselves. The bowl never runs dry, but new rocks come only when every rock is used. Every site is saved with its seed and its company's name, which is on the sign by the ledge; Sites flips between them; Start over (held) puts every rock back in the bowl.

Physics: exacting, never explosive. Convex hulls (the rock's broad shape; its fine roughness raises friction instead), a Newton contact solve with friction cones, sleeping islands, a soft hold constraint for the crane whose wires go slack when a rock is set down, and determinism (the same inputs give bit-identical results). Its acceptance tests are phys/tests.

Sound: all synthesized (audio_src/). "Stone Garden", 64 s at 60 beats a minute on struck slate bars, felt piano and a far pad. The creek: three beds of 37, 40 and 53 s (rush, babbling bubbles, wind in the reeds) that together never repeat, with water slapping stones, pebbles knocking along the bed, frogs (sometimes answered) and quiet birds at random. A faint electric whine from the crane's motor that follows its speed. Clips, knocks, a collapse, a whoosh, a rock landing in the bowl, a stalled lift, a new-best chime on stone bars, the duck's squeak.

Package: about 21 MB of sources, runtime audio and screenshots. No app bundle. The WAV music and bed masters stay on the Neo under /Users/ultimussecundai/solitaire_sounds/zen/audio_src/masters; re-render them with source/audio_src.

BUILD (standalone, verified on the Neo)
  Copy assets/ into source/assets first. The dev build reads ZC_ASSET_DIR; the bundle copies source/assets to Resources/assets.
  cmake -S source -B build -DCMAKE_PREFIX_PATH=<GUI.Forms paint-port SDK> && cmake --build build -j 8
  ctest --test-dir build      zen_tests (about 10 s): the pour, a woken heap holding, fetch/lower/release making the stack's foot,
                              a second rock joining, unwinding (only a free rock lifts), save and load, a loose rock tidied home.
                              phys_tests (about 15 s): the 16 specification tests plus feature checks. Test 16 is a timing
                              budget (see NOT VERIFIED).
  build/bench                 timings: the pour, physics in play, frames with the camera moving and still
  build/site_preview out.ppm  a headless still of the site

CODE BOUNDARY
  Everything is in namespace zc (the engine in zc::phys), so it clashes with none of games, eggy, sbx, fp, ap, pt, ld, sh, kk.
  Portable C++20 core (library zc_core, plus zc_phys):
    phys/         the engine (no dependencies)
    rocks.*, stones.*   rock generation and stone textures (pure)
    run.*         the run: bowl, crane controller, stack membership, tidying, saves (pure)
    site.*, crane_model.*, shapes.*   the scene
    platform/raster|r3d|mesh          the shared renderer. r3d now fills triangles in horizontal bands on a small pool of
                                      std::threads (up to 8) and keeps the shadow of everything that isn't moving; the output
                                      is pixel-identical to the single-threaded renderer.
  View, still portable C++ against GUI.Forms:
    zen_view.*    input, camera, HUD, Mina's lines, panels (new site, sites, help), sound, autosave
  macOS-only adapters:
    platform/text.mm     the text.hpp interface (CoreText; Georgia, Menlo)
    platform/audio.mm    the audio.hpp interface (AVFoundation)
    platform/present.mm  NativePresenter (Core Animation), plus dev_resize_window (dev only)
  main.cpp is the standalone entry point. Do not link it into Games.

INTEGRATING INTO THE CABINET
  1. Copy source/src and source/phys to vendor/zenconstruction and leave this package unchanged. Build zc_phys and zc_core plus a
     zc_ui library and link them into game_ui.
  2. Add a new collection entry (a new slot and kind; nothing is replaced). Host
       make_control<zc::ZenView>(gf::StableId("zen.view"), zc::Options{})
     below the rail like the other vendor views. Call activate() when shown. Call set_cabinet(foreground, music, sound) on tab
     changes and when the master switches change: it gates its own music and sound and stops its music when not in front. Stop the
     cabinet's music while it is in front.
  3. Pointer coordinates: on_pointer() treats e.position as relative to the control (convert under the rail, as for Eggy). Dragging
     orbits the camera, the wheel zooms, a click on a rock fetches it.
  4. Assets: add the zc_* files and zen_audio_manifest.json to assets/audio. The ui_name_* files already exist in the cabinet as AAC.
  5. Saves: zen_construction-v1.txt in the cabinet's save folder; GAMES_STATE_DIR redirects it (--dev uses
     zen_construction-dev-v1.txt). Versioned, checksummed, atomically replaced: every site (company, seed, best height, every rock's
     resting pose and place, and what rests on what).
  6. Size: the layout reflows from 600 x 370 points (the cabinet's 600 x 420 less its 50-point rail) to full screen. The scene renders
     at 1.5 points per scene pixel (1.25 when small) and is dithered up; the HUD and text are drawn at full resolution.
  7. Presentation: as with Eggy, Switchbox and Pen the Sheep. ZC_NO_NATIVE=1 forces the LiveSurface fallback.
  8. Keys: arrows, W/S, Q/E, R/F, Z/C, Shift (above); Space let go; B back to the bowl; Esc calls the crane back; H or F1 help;
     M music; [ and ] previous and next site.
  9. Dev only, with --dev: ZC_SCRIPT="t:command,..." replays commands (new=<company>, fetch, over, lower, drop, back, yaw=, pitch=,
     dist=, hold=<key>,<seconds>, help, sites, close, next, prev, startover, type=<text>, size<W>x<H>). ZC_WINDOW=WxH opens at a given
     size. ZC_PRINT_WID=1 prints the window id; with it, drawing continues while the window is covered (for captures).
     ZC_AUDIO_LOG=1 logs every sound. ZC_ARROW_ROCKS=1 paints every rock with arrows and lays an arrow on the ledge (an orientation
     check).

WINDOWS (Shadow)
  These follow the Eggy, Switchbox, Four Pegs, Atom Probe and Pen the Sheep seams in docs/WINDOWS_HANDOFF.md.
  - Replace text.mm, audio.mm, present.mm. NativePresenter::attach() may return false to use the LiveSurface fallback.
    dev_resize_window is a dev aid; a no-op is fine.
  - Audio has one seam the other games don't: audio_bed(name, gain, rate, enabled), a looping bed whose gain and playback rate
    (0.5 to 2, pitch with it) ease toward the latest call each tick (swell in about 80 ms, fall away in about 350 ms, rate glides in
    about 220 ms). Four beds: zc_rush, zc_brook, zc_reeds (constant rate) and zc_motor (rate follows the crane's speed; a 2 s WAV
    loop). audio_sfx takes a pan (-1..1) as well. audio_stop() fades everything out over about 120 ms before stopping (stopping
    players mid-wave popped the speaker on quit).
  - AAC loops must decode to exactly their manifest lengths (3,072,000 / 1,920,000 / 1,776,000 / 2,544,000 frames), as they do
    through AVAudioFile.
  - The engine is plain C++20; it asks for -ffp-contract=off (MSVC: /fp:precise) so results stay deterministic.
  - Fonts: Georgia and Menlo; on Windows Georgia is present and Consolas can stand in for Menlo.
  - M_PI needs _USE_MATH_DEFINES on MSVC. The -Wall -Wextra flags are Clang/GCC spelling. A new site is generated on std::async;
    the renderer uses std::thread. The default save root is macOS Library/Application Support; keep the file name and
    GAMES_STATE_DIR.
  - platform/raster.cpp includes the accumulate() fix, as in the other 0.4 packages.

POLICY NOTES
  - The rocks' collision shape is what the physics believes; the drawn rock never pokes outside it (at most 3 mm inside).
  - The crane's rotations go by the crane's own directions, never the camera's, so orbiting never changes what a key does.
  - All audio is synthesized: no samples, no third-party audio.
  - No copyrighted characters; the crane is an original toy-crane design after the general look of 1960s pressed-steel toys,
    with no maker's name or marks.

MEASURED / VERIFIED ON THE NEO
  - Clean build of this package's sources: OK (Release, arm64). zen_tests: PASS. phys_tests: every correctness test passes.
  - Audio: 37/37 files decode; the four AAC loops' valid-frame counts equal the manifest loop ends exactly.
  - Speed (build/bench, 733 x 507 scene pixels, the default window): frames 47 -> 20 ms with the camera moving and 33 -> 12.6 ms
    still, after the banded renderer and the kept shadows, pixel-identical output. Physics in play: about 0.01 ms a step holding a
    rock, at most 0.44 ms lowering it into contact. Pouring a new bowl of 50 rocks: about 0.25 s on a quiet machine (0.4 to 1 s
    under load), off the UI thread.
  - The package-built app was driven by ZC_SCRIPT in single runs at the default size and resized live to 600 x 370: a new site
    poured, a rock fetched and lifted, help, holding a rock in the small window. Captured in screens/ (render_crane_* is a headless
    render, not an app capture).
  - The user played the builds through the day and directed the crane (its look, lowering by line rather than boom, crane-relative
    controls, rotation), the rocks (flatter and more jagged, the textures), the creek and its sounds, and found the inside-out rock
    drawing that made rocks seem to turn with the camera (fixed).

NOT VERIFIED
  - phys_tests' test 16, a timing budget (1.5 ms mean for 75 rocks all awake), failed during packaging: 3.9 ms and 15 ms on two runs
    with the machine at a load average of 15 to 20 from other applications. When the machine was quiet it measured about 1.7 ms,
    still a little over budget; the game itself never has that many rocks awake for long. The engine's speed is the next work.
  - The final capture runs were disturbed: on a heavily loaded machine the app started late, so the shots are later than their
    labels, and keystrokes from elsewhere reached the capture window (the help panel and the music switch opened unbidden in one run).
  - Listening to the final renders: the user heard earlier builds of the sounds; the last changes (creek mix, frogs, quieter motor)
    were checked by measurement and loop seams only.
  - Reduced motion: not implemented. The cabinet's Motion master is not consumed.
  - Cabinet integration and any non-macOS platform.
